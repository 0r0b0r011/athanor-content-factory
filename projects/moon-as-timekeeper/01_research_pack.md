# Moon as Timekeeper — Research Pack

## Concept

The moon orbits Earth approximately once every 29.5 days. As it orbits, the relative positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon change, causing different portions of the Moon's sunlit half to face Earth. These changing shapes are called lunar phases.

### Core facts (for the creator, not the child)

- The Moon orbits Earth in about 27.3 days (sidereal) or 29.5 days (synodic)
- We always see the same face of the Moon (tidally locked)
- Phases cycle: new → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → full → waning gibbous → third quarter → waning crescent → new
- The terminator is the line between day and night on the Moon
- From the Northern Hemisphere, the right side of the Moon is illuminated during waxing

## Age-band profile (6-8)

- Concrete operational stage beginning
- Can classify and seriate objects
- Understands cause and effect with concrete examples
- Egocentrism decreasing; can take simple perspectives
- Literal thinking; struggles with abstract models
- Curious about natural phenomena
- Can track a simple calendar/schedule

## Misconceptions

Common:
- "The moon physically changes size/shape" — most common
- "A cloud covers part of the moon" — very common
- "The moon only comes out at night" — common
- "The moon makes its own light" — common

## Accurate model (age-appropriate)

- The moon doesn't change shape — we see different amounts of its sunlit side
- The sun always lights half the moon
- As the moon goes around Earth, we see different parts of the lit half
- Think of it like walking around a lamp: you see different shapes of light

## Emotional hooks

- Mystery: the moon "sends different letters" each night
- Pattern-seeking: children naturally love tracking patterns
- Agency: predicting the next phase is empowering
- Competence: being "someone who can read the sky"

## Symbolic opportunities

- Phase wheel (physical dial the child turns)
- Moon envelope (a container for each phase)
- Observation journal (track-and-record ritual)

## Safety constraints

- Do NOT depict night as scary or dangerous
- Do NOT suggest the moon causes bad things
- Do NOT anthropomorphise the moon as lonely (creates unnecessary melancholy)
- Keep illustrations warm, inviting, gentle
